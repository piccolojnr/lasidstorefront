# BRAND.md

Brand document for the Backthred storefront.

---

## Brand Core

Backthred is an everyday Ghanaian store for practical buying: fashion, lifestyle goods, books, and household essentials. It should feel local, trustworthy, and easy to use.

The brand is not built around prestige. It is built around familiarity, fairness, and relief. The customer should feel like they have arrived somewhere straightforward, useful, and human.

The working brand promise:

**Find what you need. Good prices. No friction.**

---

## Origin

Backthred comes from Rashid's nickname. That matters because the storefront should never read like a polished corporate marketplace with borrowed luxury cues. It should feel personal and cared for.

When choosing copy, visuals, or interaction patterns, prefer the option that feels like it came from a real shopkeeper over the option that feels like it came from a growth team.

---

## Positioning

**Backthred is:**

- Warm
- Practical
- Honest
- Affordable
- Local in feel
- Modern without being slick

**Backthred is not:**

- Luxury
- Aspirational-for-its-own-sake
- Cold
- Tech-bro retail
- Editorial fashion
- Aggressively sales-driven

---

## Audience

The storefront is for everyday shoppers in Ghana who want confidence, speed, and clarity.

They are not browsing for status. They are trying to buy useful things at fair prices from a store they can trust.

Key expectations:

- Clear prices
- Familiar language
- Easy navigation
- Fast checkout
- Visible delivery trust signals
- No intimidation

---

## Brand Feel

The emotional target is **organized ease**.

The site should feel like:

- A bright, well-run neighborhood store
- A familiar person helping you find the right shelf
- A modern checkout flow that still feels warm

The site should not feel like:

- A luxury boutique
- A cold global marketplace
- A trend-driven design exercise

---

## Voice

Backthred speaks plainly. The tone is direct, calm, and helpful.

Use:

- Short sentences
- Concrete benefits
- Everyday language
- Confidence without hype

Avoid:

- Brand theater
- Buzzwords
- Inflated claims
- Lifestyle jargon
- Fake urgency

Good examples:

- "Find what you need."
- "Good prices, trusted sellers, delivered across Ghana."
- "Start shopping in seconds."
- "Fast delivery across all 16 regions."

Bad examples:

- "Discover our curated edit."
- "Elevate your lifestyle."
- "Premium retail reimagined."
- "Shop the drop before it's gone."

---

## Visual Direction

The current storefront already points in the right direction:

- Warm cream backgrounds
- Deep brown text
- Terracotta as the main action color
- Amber as a supporting highlight
- Rounded corners without soft, playful pill shapes
- Serif headlines with clean sans-serif body text

This creates a brand that feels welcoming and grounded rather than generic.

---

## Typography

The brand typography is:

- **Headings:** Lora
- **Body/UI:** DM Sans

Why it works:

- Lora adds warmth, trust, and character
- DM Sans keeps interfaces clear and contemporary

Typography rules:

- Use serif headlines to carry personality
- Use sans-serif for navigation, forms, metadata, and body copy
- Avoid all-caps except for short labels and overlines
- Keep hierarchy obvious and readable on mobile first

---

## Color Roles

The brand palette should stay warm and earthy.

- **Background:** warm cream
- **Text:** deep warm brown
- **Primary action:** burnt terracotta
- **Support accent:** warm amber
- **Muted surfaces:** stone / warm neutral
- **Success:** earthy green

Color intent matters more than exact hex values in this document. The implementation source of truth remains the theme tokens in `src/styles/global.css`.

Rules:

- Avoid pure black and pure white
- Avoid cool greys and corporate blues
- Avoid purple gradients and generic SaaS color language
- Use terracotta for commitment actions
- Use amber as support, not as a competing primary

---

## Product and Merchandising Tone

Products should be presented clearly, not theatrically.

- Let the item name, image, and price do most of the work
- Use badges and highlights sparingly
- Keep promotional language grounded
- Make trust markers visible: authenticity, delivery, payment security

The brand should feel useful before it feels exciting.

---

## Interaction Principles

Brand and UX should reinforce each other.

- Make the next step obvious
- Keep forms simple and clearly labeled
- Reduce hesitation at checkout
- Use motion lightly and with purpose
- Prefer clarity over novelty

If an interaction is visually impressive but slows down understanding, it is off-brand.

---

## Trust Signals

Backthred should consistently reinforce a few simple promises:

- Authentic products
- Fair, visible pricing
- Secure checkout
- Delivery across Ghana
- Easy account and order tracking

These should appear as calm proof points, not loud persuasion tactics.

---

## Things To Avoid

- Luxury-brand visual codes
- Editorial minimalism that hides utility
- Generic AI storefront aesthetics
- Overwritten hero copy
- Overuse of gradients, glassmorphism, or neon accents
- Countdown timers, fake scarcity, and pushy upsells

Anything that makes the customer feel pressured, confused, or slightly unwelcome is a miss.

---

## Brand Test

Before shipping new copy or UI, ask:

1. Does this feel human?
2. Does this feel useful?
3. Does this feel welcoming to an everyday shopper in Ghana?
4. Does this help the customer decide faster?
5. Does this avoid sounding or looking performative?

If the answer is yes across those five checks, it fits the brand.
